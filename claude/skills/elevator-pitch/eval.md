# Elevator pitch eval

Run after drafting or editing a pitch. Answer each check PASS or FAIL. If FAIL, quote the problem and state the fix in one sentence. Fix all FAILs before returning the pitch.

## Structure

1. Does the first sentence open with a hook (pain point, number, or bold claim) - not context-setting, not definitions, not "In today's world"?
2. Are stakes stated - why this matters now, what breaks or stalls without action?
3. Is the mechanism specific enough to evaluate - names the what and the tech stack at minimum, not just abstract outcomes ("improves efficiency," "streamlines workflows")? Deep implementation details are NOT required and should not be present in short-form pitches.
4. Is there a clear CTA or next step the reader can act on - not "let me know your thoughts"?

## Voice

1. Does the pitch follow the `writing-style` skill's rules (banned vocabulary, structural bans, tone)?
2. Is it free of AI-slop patterns: throat-clearing openers, binary contrasts ("This is not X. It's Y."), colon reveals, fake-profound kickers, summary-recap endings, rhetorical questions as transitions, numbered-insight framing ("3 things I learned")?
3. Does it use active voice with human subjects and direct verbs - no "the integration facilitates," no inanimate actors doing human things?

## Persuasion

1. Does the pitch anticipate and address at least one obvious objection or alternative?
2. Is every claim specific and verifiable - no weasel words ("significantly improves," "many teams struggle"), no unsourced stats?
3. Does the pitch include at least one concrete number (time saved, scale, speed, cost, count)? Round estimates ("~200 repos") count; vague quantifiers ("many," "significant," "several") do not.
4. Is the length appropriate for the format - no padding, no repetition, within the target range?

## Audience fit

 1. Does the pitch match the stated audience's context - no unexplained insider terms or assumed shared background if the reader might not have it? Mechanism can go deeper for a knowledgeable/technical audience than for a stranger or a decision-maker skimming for impact.
